CHAPTER 14 — SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY REPORTING

14.2.1 A Relevant Person must establish and maintain systems and controls to identify, assess, and report suspicious activity.

14.2.3 Guidance 10. Relevant Persons should comply with guidance issued by the supervising authority regarding identifying and reporting suspicious activity and transactions relating to money laundering, terrorist financing, and proliferation financing.

14.3.1 Where a Relevant Person knows or has reasonable grounds to suspect that a transaction involves the proceeds of crime, it must make an internal report to the Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) AS SOON AS PRACTICABLE and in any event within THREE (3) BUSINESS DAYS of the suspicion arising.

14.3.4 The MLRO must consider each internal report and, where the suspicion is sustained, submit an external Suspicious Activity Report to the Financial Intelligence Unit WITHIN FIVE (5) BUSINESS DAYS of receiving the internal report.

14.5.2 A Relevant Person must not disclose to the customer or any third party that a report has been or may be made (the 'tipping-off' prohibition).

Formation pack — Firm entity-selection guidance memo

matter_documents id 235 · doc_type guidance_memo · 992 chars · family lawflow-entity-formation
ENTITY SELECTION GUIDANCE (firm standard)

Step 1 — Identify the client's financing plan. Where the client anticipates institutional venture financing, the Delaware C-Corporation is the presumptive recommendation: institutional investors typically require a Delaware C-Corp for preferred-stock issuance and 83(b)/QSBS treatment.

Step 2 — Where the client anticipates NO outside institutional financing and prioritizes pass-through taxation and operational informality, the LLC is the presumptive recommendation.

Step 3 — The S-Corporation election is unavailable where the client anticipates institutional investors, because entity shareholders and multiple classes of stock are prohibited.

Step 4 — Convertible-into-corporation LLCs are disfavored by the firm: conversion at financing triggers avoidable tax and diligence cost.

Decision rule: institutional financing anticipated within 24 months => recommend delaware_c_corp, regardless of the client's stated preference for informality.

Deadline pack — Consolidated Rules Memo: Computing Time Under the Simulated Rules of Civil Procedure (SRCP 6)

matter_documents id 146 · doc_type rules-memo · 2,063 chars · family deadline-computation
MEMORANDUM — DOCKETING STANDARDS (SIMULATED JURISDICTION)

Re: Computing every litigation deadline under SRCP 6 (this firm's controlling method)

I. The counting method. (a) Exclude the trigger day: the day of the event that starts the period is never counted. (b) Count every calendar day thereafter, including intermediate Saturdays, Sundays, and court holidays. (c) Rollover: if the last day of the period lands on a Saturday, Sunday, or court holiday, the period runs to the next day that is not a weekend day or court holiday. (d) Service adjustment: when a deadline runs from SERVICE (not filing) and service was made by mail, add 3 calendar days AFTER the period would otherwise expire; apply the rollover rule of (c) after adding the 3 days, not before.

II. Court holidays in this jurisdiction for 2026 (exhaustive list for computation): January 1 (New Year), January 19 (Civil Rights Day), May 25 (Memorial Day), July 3 (observed Independence Day), September 7 (Labor Day), November 26 (Thanksgiving), December 25 (Christmas).

III. Periods that matter here. (1) Answer to a complaint: 21 days after service of the summons and complaint. (2) Opposition to a motion: 14 days after the motion is FILED. (3) Reply in support of a motion: 7 days after the opposition is filed. (4) Notice of appeal: 30 days after entry of judgment. (5) Rule 68-style offer of judgment: acceptance within 14 days after service of the offer.

IV. Weekday calendar anchors for 2026 (verified): June 1 is a Monday; July 1 is a Wednesday; August 1 is a Saturday; September 1 is a Tuesday; October 1 is a Thursday.

V. Firm rule. Every computed date is recorded in the deadlines register as a review record whose outcome field contains ONLY the final date in YYYY-MM-DD form. A computation that ignores the rollover rule or the mail-service adjustment is a docketing error chargeable to the responsible attorney.

This memo controls over any older docketing chart. The superseded 2024 chart (which used a count-the-trigger-day method and a 5-day mail adjustment) must not be used.
