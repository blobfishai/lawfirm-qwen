**II. OUT-OF-POLICY DEVIATIONS REQUIRING ESCALATION**

**Deviation 1: Guaranteed Bonus Floor (Years 1–2)**

Dr. Chen-Velasco requests a guaranteed floor of 75% of target bonus ($181,500/yr) for the first two fiscal years, totaling $363,000 in guaranteed exposure. The CAP categorically prohibits guaranteed bonus floors for SVP-level hires — all bonuses at this level must be fully discretionary. This is a structural prohibition, not a cap with an allowable range, so no percentage deviation is calculable. The categorical nature of the prohibition means this deviation independently exceeds CPO authority regardless of dollar amount.

**Deviation 2: Change-in-Control Severance Multiplier**

The candidate requests a 2.0× (base + target bonus) CIC severance package, which equals 2.0 × $682,000 = $1,364,000. The CAP SVP limit is 1.0× (base + target bonus) = $682,000. This produces an incremental CIC cash exposure of $682,000 — a +100% deviation above the CAP limit, which independently exceeds the 25% CPO threshold and requires Board Compensation Committee approval under CAP §4.3.

**Deviation 3: CIC Double-Trigger Equity Acceleration**

The candidate requests 100% acceleration of unvested RSUs upon a qualifying CIC event. The CAP SVP limit is 50% of unvested. In the worst-case scenario — a CIC occurring at day 366 after the one-year cliff — the unvested portion would be 75% of $2,200,000 = $1,650,000, producing an incremental acceleration exposure of 50% × $1,650,000 = $825,000.

Combined maximum CIC exposure from Deviations 2 and 3: $682,000 + $825,000 = **$1,507,000** above policy.

**Additional Out-of-Policy Item: Non-CIC Severance**

For completeness, the candidate also requests 12 months of non-CIC severance ($440,000) versus the CAP provision of 9 months at the policy base ($318,750). This is a deviation of +$121,250 (+38%), which exceeds the 25% CPO threshold and should be addressed in the escalation memo as well.

**Aggregate Exposure and Threshold Analysis**

Total incremental economic exposure across all deviations: $363,000 + $682,000 + $825,000 = **$1,870,000**. While this aggregate figure falls below the Board Compensation Committee's $2,000,000 economic threshold under CAP §4.3, the CIC severance deviation alone exceeds the 25% percentage deviation threshold, which independently triggers Board Compensation Committee approval. Board Compensation Committee approval is therefore required for the CIC-related terms (Deviations 2 and 3), and given the interrelated nature of the full package, I believe it is prudent to present all out-of-policy deviations to the Committee for a comprehensive review.

- **CIC severance multiplier:** Peer median is 1.5×; the 75th percentile is 2.0×. The candidate's request of 2.0× is at the market ceiling.

- **CIC equity acceleration:** 7 of 18 peers (39%) provide 100% double-trigger acceleration. The peer median is 75%. The candidate's request of 100% is above the median but within the upper quartile.

These data points should inform the recommended positions and give the Committee appropriate market context for their decision.

The Committee should understand that this hire is not routine — it is foundational to a critical value-creation pillar.

**(6) Propose a Recommended Position for Each Deviation**

For each of the three primary deviations and the non-CIC severance issue, the memo should present one of three options — **accept**, **counter**, or **reject** — with clear rationale for the recommendation. Recommendations should be informed by the Whitfield Greer market data, the strategic business case, the risk analysis, and the time constraints we are operating under.

Where countering, propose specific alternative terms with supporting logic. For example:

- A guaranteed bonus floor closer to the peer median (e.g., 50% of target, Year 1 only) rather than 75% for two years.
- A 1.5× CIC severance multiplier in line with the peer median rather than 2.0×.
- 75% CIC equity acceleration matching the peer median rather than 100%.

For any deviations where you recommend acceptance, address how the approval can be structured to limit precedent — one-time deviation approval language, documentation of candidate-specific factors that justify the exception, sunset provisions, or other containment mechanisms. The Committee will want assurance that approving these terms does not create a new baseline for future hires.
