# legalbench-rule-application pack — Firm rule memo: Personal jurisdiction and diversity jurisdiction tests

WHITLOCK & DEENE LLP — INTERNAL RULE MEMORANDUM (Civil Procedure Group)
Re: Forum-selection screening: personal jurisdiction and diversity jurisdiction

PART I — PERSONAL JURISDICTION.
A forum state has personal jurisdiction over a defendant in either of two ways.
(1) General jurisdiction: the defendant is domiciled in the forum state. An individual is domiciled where they reside with intent to remain; a corporation is at home in its state of incorporation and the state of its principal place of business.
(2) Specific jurisdiction: BOTH prongs below must be satisfied —
  (a) Purposeful contacts: the defendant has sufficient contacts with the forum such that it has purposefully availed itself of the privileges of conducting activities there. Contacts created unilaterally by the plaintiff, and merely fortuitous presence such as passing through the state in transit on unrelated business, do not count.
  (b) Nexus: the plaintiff's claim arises out of or relates to those forum contacts.
Firm rule of decision: if the defendant is not domiciled in the forum and either prong (a) or prong (b) fails, personal jurisdiction is lacking. A defendant who repeatedly traveled into the forum to solicit and negotiate the very contract being sued upon satisfies both prongs. A defendant with no operations, customers, or purposeful activity in the forum — whose only connection is unrelated transit — satisfies neither.

PART II — DIVERSITY JURISDICTION.
A federal court has diversity jurisdiction when BOTH requirements are met:
(1) Complete diversity: no plaintiff is a citizen of the same state as any defendant. A corporation is a citizen of every state where it is incorporated AND the state of its principal place of business.
(2) Amount in controversy: the amount in controversy must EXCEED $75,000, exclusive of interest and costs. The firm's settled position, adopted after the Calloway remand: a demand of exactly $75,000.00 does NOT satisfy the requirement, because the statute requires that the matter in controversy exceed the sum of $75,000. Associates must not round in the client's favor; exactly $75,000 fails.
Both requirements are independent. Complete diversity with an insufficient amount fails; a large amount without complete diversity fails.

PART III — WORKFLOW. When screening an intake letter, resolve each numbered scenario separately, apply only the tests above, and record one determination per scenario. This memo supersedes all earlier jurisdiction threshold drafts, including any draft referencing a $50,000 threshold or a 'doing business' test; those drafts are withdrawn and must not be cited.
