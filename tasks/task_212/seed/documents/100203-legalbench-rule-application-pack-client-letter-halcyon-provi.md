# legalbench-rule-application pack — Client letter: Halcyon Provisions acquisitions and brand clearance questions

HALCYON PROVISIONS COMPANY
Providence, Rhode Island — Legal Operations

To the M&A and Brands teams, Whitlock & Deene LLP:

Two groups of questions from our expansion program. Please handle each numbered item independently under the applicable firm memo and record determinations as your assignment instructions direct.

PART ONE — SUCCESSOR LIABILITY SCREENING.

ACQUISITION 1 — Pellworth Canning. Two years ago Halcyon purchased substantially all of the assets of Pellworth Canning Co. Section 7.2 of the executed asset purchase agreement states that the purchaser 'expressly assumes all product liability obligations of Seller, whether arising before or after Closing.' A consumer class has now sued Halcyon over defective seams in cans Pellworth manufactured before the closing — squarely a product liability claim of the seller. We need a determination whether successor exposure attaches to Halcyon for this claim.

ACQUISITION 2 — Dunlow Cold Storage. Last year Halcyon purchased the warehouse racking, refrigeration units, and delivery fleet of Dunlow Cold Storage LLC in an arm's-length sale at a price our banker certified as fair market value. The purchase agreement contains NO assumption of liabilities of any kind. Dunlow remained in existence after the closing, retained its corporate registration, and applied the sale proceeds to its creditors. No officer, director, or shareholder of Dunlow holds any position or share in Halcyon, and Halcyon has never held itself out as Dunlow. A vendor with an old unpaid invoice against Dunlow now demands payment from Halcyon. We need a determination whether successor exposure attaches to Halcyon for this claim.

PART TWO — BRAND CLEARANCE (Abercrombie classification).

MARK 1 — QUELLIVAR. Our agency coined the term QUELLIVAR for our new canned soup line. The term was invented from scratch for this project; it has no prior meaning in English or in any other language, appears in no dictionary, and was verified by the agency as a pure coinage with no existing usage anywhere.

MARK 2 — CRISPCRUST. For our frozen pizza line we propose the mark CRISPCRUST. The product's defining feature, confirmed by our test kitchen, is its notably crisp crust, and the name was chosen precisely because it directly states that feature to the shopper. A consumer reading the mark on the freezer door understands immediately, without any imagination or inference, that the pizza has a crisp crust.

Please log one determination for each acquisition and one classification record for each mark.
