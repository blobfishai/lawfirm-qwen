# legalbench-rule-application pack — Firm rule memo: Successor liability and its four exceptions

WHITLOCK & DEENE LLP — INTERNAL RULE MEMORANDUM (M&A / Litigation Interface Group)
Re: Successor liability screening for asset acquisitions

Section 1. The general rule. When one company purchases the assets of another, the purchaser is generally NOT liable for the seller's debts and liabilities. Asset deals are therefore the default structure for liability containment. The general rule yields only where one of four recognized exceptions applies.

Section 2. The four exceptions.
(1) Express agreement: the purchaser expressly agrees in the transaction documents to assume the seller's liabilities. An assumption clause covering the relevant class of liabilities is sufficient by itself; no other factor is needed.
(2) Fraudulent conveyance: the assets are transferred to the purchaser in order to escape liability — indicators include inadequate consideration, a seller left insolvent and unable to satisfy known claims, and awareness of looming claims coupled with a structure designed to strand them.
(3) De facto merger: the transaction is a merger in substance — continuity of ownership between seller shareholders and purchaser, cessation and dissolution of the seller, assumption by the purchaser of the operations needed to continue the business, and continuity of management.
(4) Mere continuation: the purchaser is the same enterprise wearing a new coat — common officers, directors, or shareholders, and only one corporation remaining after the transfer.

Section 3. What is NOT an exception. The firm's settled screening positions: (a) the purchaser's mere knowledge of the seller's potential liabilities, without more, triggers no exception; (b) payment of fair market value in an arm's-length sale negates the fraudulent conveyance indicators; (c) where the seller corporation survives the closing, retains its separate existence, and applies the sale proceeds to its creditors, neither de facto merger nor mere continuation is available; (d) absence of any shared officers, directors, or shareholders defeats mere continuation. A transaction bearing all of features (b), (c), and (d), with no assumption clause, falls under the general rule: the purchaser takes free of the seller's liabilities.

Section 4. Recording discipline. Screening determinations are recorded per scenario in the matter system. A determination that at least one exception applies (successor exposure attaches) is the affirmative disposition; a determination that the general rule controls (no exception applies) is the negative disposition. The assignment instructions control the exact recording strings. Always name the operative exception, if any, in your rationale.
