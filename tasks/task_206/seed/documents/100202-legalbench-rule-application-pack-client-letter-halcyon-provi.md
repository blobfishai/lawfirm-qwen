# legalbench-rule-application pack — Client letter: Halcyon Provisions commercial contract portfolio (Article 2 scoping)

HALCYON PROVISIONS COMPANY
Providence, Rhode Island — Legal Operations

To the Commercial Group, Whitlock & Deene LLP:

Ahead of our annual audit we need a governing-law scoping determination — UCC Article 2 or common law — for three contracts in our active portfolio. Please analyze each contract separately under the firm's Article 2 scope memo and record one determination per contract as your assignment instructions direct. The recitals below are complete and accurate; no other terms bear on classification.

CONTRACT 1 — Bramblewick Orchards supply agreement. Halcyon agrees to purchase, and Bramblewick Orchards LLC agrees to sell and deliver, 1,200 cases of jarred stone-fruit preserves, packed to our label specification, for a total price of $86,400. Title to the cases passes to Halcyon on delivery at our Providence warehouse. The contract contains no service obligations of any kind beyond ordinary delivery of the cases.

CONTRACT 2 — Ferrand & Slate advisory engagement. Halcyon engages the consulting firm Ferrand & Slate Partners to study and redesign our warehouse picking workflow, deliver a written recommendations report, and coach our floor supervisors through implementation, for a professional fee of $54,000. Ferrand & Slate sells us no equipment and transfers title to nothing except the paper report embodying its advice. The entire subject of the engagement is advisory and design labor.

CONTRACT 3 — Marovic Equipment bottling line agreement. Halcyon agrees to buy a model MX-9 automated bottling line from Marovic Equipment Corp. for a single total contract price of $290,000. The contract itself allocates the price: $248,000 is attributable to the bottling line machinery (the goods component), and $42,000 is attributable to on-site installation, calibration, and operator training (the services component). It is a single indivisible contract; the parties did not execute separate agreements.

For the avoidance of doubt: Contract 1 involves no real estate; Contract 2 involves no transfer of goods beyond the report paper; Contract 3's allocation figures above are the ones our auditors have certified.

Regards,
Sybil Antczak, Director of Legal Operations
Halcyon Provisions Company
