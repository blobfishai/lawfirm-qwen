**DEVIATION 1: GUARANTEED BONUS FLOOR (YEARS 1–2)**

**Candidate request:** Guaranteed minimum bonus of 75% of target bonus ($181,500/yr) for the first two fiscal years. Total guaranteed bonus exposure: $181,500 × 2 = **$363,000**.

**CAP position:** Guaranteed bonus floors for SVP-level hires are categorically prohibited under the CAP. All bonuses at this level must be fully discretionary. This is a structural prohibition, not a numeric cap with an allowable deviation range — no percentage deviation is calculable against a zero baseline.

**Peer benchmarking (Whitfield Greer report, April 2025):** Only 4 of 18 peer companies (22%) in the enterprise SaaS $1B–$3B valuation peer group offer guaranteed bonus floors. The peer median among those that do is 50% of target in Year 1 only. Dr. Chen-Velasco's request of 75% for two years sits at the 90th percentile of the peer group.

Because this is a categorical prohibition rather than a numeric cap, I am flagging it as requiring escalation beyond my authority.

**For GC attention:** Samuel, I would appreciate your assessment of the potential Section 409A deferred compensation characterization of a guaranteed bonus floor of this nature. The structure may implicate short-term deferral exemption timing requirements depending on how payment terms are documented.

---

**DEVIATION 2: CHANGE-IN-CONTROL SEVERANCE MULTIPLIER**

**Candidate request:** 2.0× (base salary + target bonus) CIC severance = 2.0 × ($440,000 + $242,000) = 2.0 × $682,000 = **$1,364,000**.

**CAP limit:** 1.0× (base + target bonus). At the candidate's requested compensation level, CAP CIC severance = 1.0 × $682,000 = $682,000. At policy-level compensation, CAP CIC severance = 1.0 × ($425,000 + $212,500) = $637,500.

**Incremental CIC severance exposure:** $1,364,000 − $682,000 = **$682,000** above policy.

**Percentage deviation:** +100% above CAP limit. This independently exceeds the 25% threshold requiring Compensation Committee approval under CAP § 4.3.

**Peer benchmarking:** The peer median CIC multiplier is 1.5×; the 75th percentile is 2.0×. The candidate's request of 2.0× is at the market ceiling / 75th percentile — aggressive but not unprecedented.

**Critical flag — Ridgeline Partners:** I want to underscore that Ridgeline Partners, Inc. (our investment bankers) has indicated a potential IPO or strategic transaction within 18–24 months. This makes CIC provisions more than theoretical and materially increases the probability that these terms could actually be triggered. This significantly elevates the urgency of proper authorization and the importance of getting the economic exposure right.

**For GC attention:** Samuel, this deviation also raises potential Section 280G golden parachute excise tax exposure. I would appreciate your assessment of the 280G implications given the Ridgeline Partners timeline, including whether a cutback provision or shareholder vote mechanism should be incorporated into the offer terms.

**DEVIATION 3: CIC DOUBLE-TRIGGER EQUITY ACCELERATION**

**Candidate request:** 100% acceleration of unvested RSUs upon a qualifying CIC event (double-trigger).

**CAP limit:** 50% acceleration of unvested RSUs upon a qualifying CIC event (double-trigger).

**Worst-case incremental exposure:** If a CIC event occurs at day 366 (just after the 1-year cliff), 75% of the $2,200,000 grant remains unvested = $1,650,000. The incremental acceleration beyond CAP = 50% × $1,650,000 = **$825,000**.

**Peer benchmarking:** 7 of 18 peers (39%) provide 100% double-trigger acceleration; the peer median is 75%. The candidate's request of 100% is above median but within the upper quartile of the peer group.
