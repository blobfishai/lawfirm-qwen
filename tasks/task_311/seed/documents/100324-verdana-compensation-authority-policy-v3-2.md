Article 1 — Purpose and Scope
Section 1.1 — Statement of Purpose. The purpose of this Compensation Authority Policy (the "Policy") is to establish maximum compensation authority parameters ("CAP Limits") for all executive-level employment offers, employment agreements, retention packages, and severance arrangements entered into by Verdana Technologies, Inc. ("Verdana" or the "Company"), a Delaware C-Corporation headquartered in Austin, Texas. This Policy sets forth the framework within which the Company's management, People Operations function, and external advisors must operate when structuring, negotiating, and finalizing compensation arrangements for senior executives, and defines the escalation procedures required when proposed arrangements would exceed the limits established herein.
Section 1.2 — Scope of Application. This Policy applies to all offers, employment agreements, retention packages, severance arrangements, and any other compensation-related commitments made to individuals being recruited for, promoted into, or currently occupying positions at the following executive levels across all divisions, subsidiaries, and affiliates of the Company:
(a) Vice President ("VP");
(b) Senior Vice President ("SVP"); and
(c) C-Suite (Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial Officer, Chief Technology Officer, Chief Operating Officer, Chief People Officer, Chief Revenue Officer, Chief Legal Officer, and any other officer holding a "Chief" title or equivalent designation as determined by the Board of Directors).
For avoidance of doubt, this Policy governs compensation commitments made both to external candidates and to internal employees being promoted or renegotiating existing arrangements.
Section 1.3 — Supersession. This Policy supersedes all prior compensation authority policies, memoranda, guidelines, and related governance documents of the Company, including without limitation CAP v.3.1 (approved September 12, 2024) and all earlier versions. To the extent that any previously approved employment arrangement contains terms that were authorized under a prior version of this Policy, such terms shall remain in effect for the duration of the applicable employment agreement, but any modification, extension, or renegotiation of such terms shall be governed by this Policy.
Section 1.4 — Binding Effect. This Policy is binding on all Company officers, members of the People Operations function (including recruiters, compensation analysts, and human resources business partners), hiring managers at every level, and any external advisors, executive search firms, or legal counsel engaged by the Company to negotiate employment terms on behalf of Verdana. No individual subject to this Policy may commit the Company to any compensation term that exceeds the applicable CAP Limit without obtaining the requisite approval in accordance with Article 4.
Section 1.5 — Policy Objectives. The objectives of this Policy are as follows:
(a) Consistency. To ensure that executive compensation decisions are made within a consistent and transparent framework, reducing the risk of ad hoc commitments that may create internal equity concerns or set unintended precedents.
(b) Fiduciary Prudence. To ensure that the Company's compensation commitments are consistent with sound fiduciary principles, particularly in light of the Company's obligations to its stockholders and investors.
(c) Governance Compliance. To establish clear lines of authority and escalation, ensuring that material compensation decisions receive appropriate Board-level oversight and that all arrangements comply with applicable legal and regulatory requirements.
(d) Management Flexibility. To preserve the ability of the Company's management to compete effectively for top talent within defined guardrails, recognizing that rigid inflexibility in executive compensation can be as detrimental to the Company's interests as unconstrained spending authority.
Section 1.6 — Competitive Context. The Company recognizes that Verdana, with approximately 2,400 employees and a pre-IPO valuation of $1.8 billion as established in the Series E financing round, operates in an intensely competitive talent market for enterprise SaaS executives. The CAP Limits established in this Policy are designed to reflect that competitive environment, informed by peer benchmarking data compiled by the Company's independent compensation consultant and reviewed by the Board Compensation Committee. These limits are intended to position the Company competitively at or above the 75th percentile of the relevant peer group for total compensation, while maintaining internal equity and governance discipline commensurate with the Company's stage, scale, and anticipated path toward a public offering.
Article 2 — Definitions
As used in this Policy, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth below. Capitalized terms used but not defined in this Article shall have the meanings ascribed to them elsewhere in this Policy or, if not defined herein, in the Company's Equity Incentive Plan.
"Base Salary" means the annualized fixed cash compensation payable to the executive, exclusive of any bonus, equity-based compensation, benefits, perquisites, and any other forms of compensation. Base Salary shall be determined as of the date of an employment offer or, in the case of a renegotiation, as of the effective date of the amendment.
"Board Compensation Committee" or "Committee" means the Compensation Committee of the Board of Directors of Verdana Technologies, Inc., as constituted from time to time in accordance with the Committee's charter.
"CAP Limit" means the maximum value or term specified in Article 3 of this Policy for each compensation element at each executive level. CAP Limits may be expressed as dollar amounts, percentages, multiples, durations, or structural prohibitions, as applicable.
"Change in Control" or "CIC" means a "Change in Control" as defined in the Company's Equity Incentive Plan, as in effect on the date of the applicable employment offer or agreement. As of the date of this Policy, the Equity Incentive Plan generally defines a Change in Control to include any merger, consolidation, acquisition, asset sale, or similar transaction resulting in a change of more than fifty percent (50%) of the beneficial ownership of the Company's outstanding voting securities or a change of more than fifty percent (50%) of the composition of the Board of Directors occurring other than through Board-approved elections.
"CIC Severance" means cash severance compensation payable to an executive upon a qualifying termination of employment occurring in connection with, or within a specified period following, a Change in Control, as set forth in the applicable employment agreement or severance plan.
"CIC Severance Multiplier" means the multiple applied to the sum of the executive's Base Salary and Target Annual Bonus to determine the amount of CIC Severance payable. For example, a CIC Severance Multiplier of 1.0× applied to a Base Salary of $425,000 and a Target Annual Bonus of $212,500 would yield CIC Severance of $637,500.
"CPO" means the Chief People Officer of Verdana Technologies, Inc. As of the date of this Policy, the CPO is Priya Ramanathan.
"Deviation" means any proposed employment term that exceeds the applicable CAP Limit, whether in dollar amount, percentage, duration, multiplier, structural design, or any other dimension. A Deviation includes, without limitation, any proposed departure from a categorical prohibition established in this Policy (such as the prohibition on Guaranteed Bonus Floors), regardless of the magnitude of the departure.
"Double-Trigger Acceleration" means acceleration of unvested equity awards upon a qualifying termination of employment occurring within a specified period (as set forth in the applicable employment agreement or equity award agreement) following a Change in Control. Double-Trigger Acceleration requires both the occurrence of a CIC and a qualifying termination; neither event alone is sufficient to trigger acceleration.
"Economic Exposure" means the estimated maximum incremental cost to the Company of a Deviation, calculated as the difference between the proposed term and the applicable CAP Limit, aggregated across all related Deviations within a single employment arrangement. Economic Exposure is calculated on a pre-tax basis and includes the estimated value of both cash and equity-based commitments. For equity-based Deviations, Economic Exposure shall be determined using grant-date fair value methodologies consistent with the Company's financial reporting practices.
"GC" means the General Counsel of Verdana Technologies, Inc. As of the date of this Policy, the GC is Samuel T. Brockington.
"Guaranteed Bonus Floor" means any contractual minimum payout under an annual incentive plan, regardless of individual or Company performance, including but not limited to (a) minimum guaranteed percentages of Target Annual Bonus, (b) fixed-dollar minimum payouts, (c) contractual provisions ensuring that an executive will receive no less than a specified amount or percentage of the Target Annual Bonus regardless of performance against applicable objectives, and (d) any other arrangement or commitment that has the economic effect of guaranteeing a minimum level of annual incentive compensation.
"Initial Equity Grant" means the aggregate grant-date fair value of equity awards (including restricted stock units ("RSUs"), stock options, performance stock units ("PSUs"), or other equity-based instruments) made to an executive in connection with the executive's commencement of employment with the Company. For purposes of this Policy, Initial Equity Grant includes any equity awards promised or committed in an offer letter or employment agreement as part of the new-hire compensation package, regardless of whether such awards are granted on the executive's start date or on a subsequent grant date within the first ninety (90) days of employment.
"Non-CIC Severance" means cash severance compensation payable to an executive upon a qualifying termination of employment not occurring in connection with a Change in Control, as set forth in the applicable employment agreement or severance plan.
"Restrictive Covenant Period" means the post-termination period during which non-competition, non-solicitation, and non-disparagement obligations, as set forth in the applicable employment agreement or restrictive covenant agreement, remain in effect and binding upon the executive.
"Single-Trigger Acceleration" means acceleration of unvested equity awards upon the occurrence of a Change in Control, without requiring a qualifying termination of employment. Single-Trigger Acceleration is triggered solely by the occurrence of the CIC event itself.
"Target Annual Bonus" means the annual incentive compensation target established for the executive, expressed as a percentage of Base Salary, payable upon achievement of performance objectives established by the Company in its sole discretion. The Target Annual Bonus represents a target-level payout; actual payouts may range from zero to above-target levels based on performance, subject to the terms of the applicable incentive plan.
Article 3 — Compensation Limits by Executive Level
Section 3.1 — Vice President Level
The following CAP Limits apply to all employment offers, agreements, and arrangements for positions at the Vice President level:
3.1.1 — Base Salary. The maximum permissible Base Salary for any VP-level position is Three Hundred Fifty Thousand Dollars ($350,000) per year.
3.1.2 — Target Annual Bonus. The maximum permissible Target Annual Bonus for any VP-level position is forty percent (40%) of Base Salary. At the CAP Base Salary limit of $350,000, this equates to a maximum Target Annual Bonus of $140,000 per year.
3.1.3 — Guaranteed Bonus Floor. Guaranteed Bonus Floors are categorically prohibited at the VP level. All annual incentive compensation at the VP level must be fully discretionary and payable solely upon achievement of performance objectives established by the Company in its sole discretion. No contractual minimum payout — whether expressed as a percentage of Target Annual Bonus, a fixed dollar amount, or any other formulation — is permitted. The CAP baseline for any Guaranteed Bonus Floor at the VP level is zero dollars ($0).
3.1.4 — Initial Equity Grant. The maximum permissible aggregate grant-date fair value of the Initial Equity Grant for any VP-level position is One Million Two Hundred Thousand Dollars ($1,200,000). Initial Equity Grants at the VP level shall consist of RSUs subject to the Company's standard four-year vesting schedule with a one-year cliff, unless an alternative vesting schedule is approved in accordance with Article 4.
3.1.5 — CIC Severance Multiplier. The maximum permissible CIC Severance Multiplier for any VP-level position is 0.75× the sum of Base Salary and Target Annual Bonus.
3.1.6 — CIC Equity Acceleration — Single-Trigger. Single-Trigger Acceleration is not available at the VP level. The maximum permissible Single-Trigger Acceleration at the VP level is zero percent (0%) of unvested equity awards. No Single-Trigger Acceleration may be offered, committed, or agreed to for any VP-level executive.
3.1.7 — CIC Equity Acceleration — Double-Trigger. The maximum permissible Double-Trigger Acceleration for any VP-level position is twenty-five percent (25%) of unvested equity awards.
3.1.8 — Non-CIC Severance. The maximum permissible Non-CIC Severance for any VP-level position is six (6) months of Base Salary.
3.1.9 — Restrictive Covenant Period. The standard Restrictive Covenant Period for any VP-level position is twelve (12) months post-termination, encompassing non-competition, non-solicitation, and non-disparagement obligations. The restrictive covenant terms and enforceability considerations shall be reviewed by the General Counsel for compliance with applicable state law.
Section 3.2 — Senior Vice President Level
The following CAP Limits apply to all employment offers, agreements, and arrangements for positions at the Senior Vice President level:
3.2.1 — Base Salary. The maximum permissible Base Salary for any SVP-level position is Four Hundred Twenty-Five Thousand Dollars ($425,000) per year. This limit reflects the Company's assessment of competitive market positioning for SVP-level enterprise SaaS executives, informed by peer benchmarking data reviewed by the Board Compensation Committee. Actual offers should target the median of the applicable peer group and should approach the CAP Limit only where competitive conditions, candidate qualifications, or retention considerations documented in writing warrant positioning above the median.
3.2.2 — Target Annual Bonus. The maximum permissible Target Annual Bonus for any SVP-level position is fifty percent (50%) of Base Salary. At the CAP Base Salary limit of $425,000, this equates to a maximum Target Annual Bonus of Two Hundred Twelve Thousand Five Hundred Dollars ($212,500) per year. The Target Annual Bonus is a target-level payout; the actual annual incentive award may range from zero to above-target levels based on the executive's achievement of individual and Company performance objectives established by the Company in its sole discretion.
3.2.3 — Guaranteed Bonus Floor. Guaranteed Bonus Floors are categorically prohibited at the SVP level. All annual incentive compensation at the SVP level must be fully discretionary and payable solely upon achievement of performance objectives established by the Company in its sole discretion. No guaranteed minimum payout — whether expressed as a percentage of Target Annual Bonus, a fixed dollar amount, or any other formulation — is permitted at the SVP level. The CAP baseline for any Guaranteed Bonus Floor at the SVP level (and at all other levels governed by this Policy) is zero dollars ($0).
This prohibition is structural in nature, not a numerical cap subject to deviation within an allowable range. Because the CAP baseline for a Guaranteed Bonus Floor is $0, any departure from this prohibition — regardless of the dollar amount or percentage involved — constitutes a Deviation that cannot be approved by the CPO under Tier 1 authority and must be escalated to the Board Compensation Committee under Article 4, Section 4.3. The establishment of a Guaranteed Bonus Floor of any magnitude represents a fundamental departure from the Company's incentive compensation philosophy, which is predicated on pay-for-performance alignment and full management discretion over annual incentive payouts.
3.2.4 — Initial Equity Grant. The maximum permissible aggregate grant-date fair value of the Initial Equity Grant for any SVP-level position is Two Million Dollars ($2,000,000). Initial Equity Grants at the SVP level shall consist of RSUs subject to the Company's standard four-year vesting schedule with a one-year cliff, unless an alternative equity vehicle or vesting schedule is approved in accordance with Article 4 and the terms of the Company's Equity Incentive Plan.
3.2.5 — CIC Severance Multiplier. The maximum permissible CIC Severance Multiplier for any SVP-level position is 1.0× the sum of Base Salary and Target Annual Bonus. At the CAP limits of $425,000 Base Salary and $212,500 Target Annual Bonus, this equates to a maximum CIC Severance payment of Six Hundred Thirty-Seven Thousand Five Hundred Dollars ($637,500). CIC Severance at the SVP level shall be payable upon a qualifying termination (involuntary termination without cause or resignation for good reason) occurring within a specified period following a Change in Control, as set forth in the applicable employment agreement.
3.2.6 — CIC Equity Acceleration — Single-Trigger. The maximum permissible Single-Trigger Acceleration for any SVP-level position is twenty-five percent (25%) of unvested equity awards, effective upon the occurrence of a qualifying Change in Control event without requiring a qualifying termination of employment. The inclusion of Single-Trigger Acceleration at the SVP level represents an acknowledgment that SVP-level executives may experience significant disruption in connection with a CIC transaction. However, Single-Trigger Acceleration is limited to 25% in order to preserve retention incentives through the post-transaction integration period.
3.2.7 — CIC Equity Acceleration — Double-Trigger. The maximum permissible Double-Trigger Acceleration for any SVP-level position is fifty percent (50%) of unvested equity awards, effective upon a qualifying termination of employment occurring within a specified period following a Change in Control. The Company's maximum permissible commitment for Double-Trigger Acceleration at the SVP level is 50% of unvested equity awards. Any request for acceleration exceeding 50% — including, without limitation, requests for 75% or 100% of unvested equity awards — constitutes a Deviation above the CAP Limit. In the case of a request for 100% Double-Trigger Acceleration (i.e., full acceleration), the Deviation is 50 percentage points above the CAP Limit (representing a 100% increase over the 50% limit), which exceeds the 25% threshold for CPO authority under Section 4.1 and must therefore be escalated to the Board Compensation Committee under Section 4.3.
3.2.8 — Non-CIC Severance. The maximum permissible Non-CIC Severance for any SVP-level position is nine (9) months of Base Salary. At the CAP Base Salary limit of $425,000, this equates to a maximum Non-CIC Severance payment of Three Hundred Eighteen Thousand Seven Hundred Fifty Dollars ($318,750), calculated as nine (9) monthly installments of $35,416.67 (rounded). Non-CIC Severance shall be conditioned upon the executive's execution and non-revocation of a general release of claims in a form satisfactory to the Company.
3.2.9 — Restrictive Covenant Period. The standard Restrictive Covenant Period for any SVP-level position is twelve (12) months post-termination, encompassing non-competition, non-solicitation, and non-disparagement obligations. The scope, geographic limitations, and enforceability of restrictive covenant provisions shall be reviewed by the General Counsel for compliance with applicable state and federal law, including any applicable state-specific restrictions on non-competition agreements.
Section 3.3 — C-Suite Level
The following CAP Limits apply to all employment offers, agreements, and arrangements for C-Suite-level positions, with the exception of the Chief Executive Officer, whose compensation is governed separately and exclusively by the Board Compensation Committee:
3.3.1 — Base Salary. The maximum permissible Base Salary for any C-Suite-level position (other than the CEO) is Six Hundred Thousand Dollars ($600,000) per year. CEO compensation, including Base Salary, is determined solely by the Board Compensation Committee and is not subject to the CAP Limits set forth in this Policy.
3.3.2 — Target Annual Bonus. The maximum permissible Target Annual Bonus for any C-Suite-level position (other than the CEO) is seventy-five percent (75%) of Base Salary. At the CAP Base Salary limit of $600,000, this equates to a maximum Target Annual Bonus of Four Hundred Fifty Thousand Dollars ($450,000) per year.
3.3.3 — Guaranteed Bonus Floor. Guaranteed Bonus Floors are categorically prohibited at the C-Suite level, consistent with the Company-wide prohibition applicable at every executive level governed by this Policy. All annual incentive compensation at the C-Suite level must be fully discretionary and payable solely upon achievement of performance objectives in the Company's sole discretion. No contractual minimum payout is permitted at any level, including C-Suite. The structural and escalation provisions set forth in Section 3.2.3 apply with equal force at the C-Suite level.
3.3.4 — Initial Equity Grant. The maximum permissible aggregate grant-date fair value of the Initial Equity Grant for any C-Suite-level position (other than the CEO) is Four Million Dollars ($4,000,000). Initial Equity Grants at the C-Suite level may consist of RSUs, performance stock units ("PSUs"), or a combination thereof, with a vesting schedule approved by the Board Compensation Committee. PSU awards at the C-Suite level shall include performance conditions aligned with the Company's strategic objectives as determined by the Committee.
3.3.5 — CIC Severance Multiplier. The maximum permissible CIC Severance Multiplier for any C-Suite-level position (other than the CEO) is 1.5× the sum of Base Salary and Target Annual Bonus. At the CAP limits of $600,000 Base Salary and $450,000 Target Annual Bonus, this equates to a maximum CIC Severance payment of One Million Five Hundred Seventy-Five Thousand Dollars ($1,575,000).
3.3.6 — CIC Equity Acceleration — Single-Trigger. The maximum permissible Single-Trigger Acceleration for any C-Suite-level position (other than the CEO) is fifty percent (50%) of unvested equity awards.
3.3.7 — CIC Equity Acceleration — Double-Trigger. The maximum permissible Double-Trigger Acceleration for any C-Suite-level position (other than the CEO) is seventy-five percent (75%) of unvested equity awards.
