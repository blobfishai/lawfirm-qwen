# MAUD deal points pack — client alert: appraisal rights under Section 262 DGCL in two-step mergers (general guidance)

CLIENT ALERT — Ellsworth & Crane LLP, M&A practice group. Appraisal rights under Section 262 of the Delaware General Corporation Law in two-step tender-offer mergers: a refresher for deal teams. This alert is general guidance; it does not describe the terms of any particular pending transaction.

1. Availability. In a merger effected under Section 251(h), stockholders who do not tender and who perfect their rights under Section 262 may seek a judicial appraisal of the fair value of their shares. Because no stockholder vote occurs, the demand mechanics run from the notice of the merger rather than from a meeting record date, and strict compliance with the statutory timeline remains a trap for the unwary.

2. Appraisal closing conditions generally. Acquirors sometimes negotiate a condition that the offer need not be consummated if appraisal demands exceed a negotiated ceiling. In our recent experience, such ceilings most commonly fall at ten percent (10%) or twelve percent (12%) of outstanding shares, though both higher and lower formulations appear; many strategic buyers dispense with the condition entirely, and its presence, threshold and measurement date vary deal by deal. Whether any given agreement contains such a condition — and at what percentage — can be determined only from that agreement's conditions article.

3. Interaction with fee provisions. An appraisal condition is a closing condition, not a deal-protection device; its failure typically permits termination without any termination fee becoming payable, subject to the agreement's effect-of-termination section.

4. Practice pointers. (a) Diligence the holder base early: concentrated event-driven funds correlate with appraisal risk. (b) Model the interest-rate arbitrage under the post-2016 amendments, including the prepayment mechanic of Section 262(h). (c) In drafting, define Dissenting Shares by reference to demands properly exercised and not withdrawn or lost, and fix the measurement time at the offer's expiration.
