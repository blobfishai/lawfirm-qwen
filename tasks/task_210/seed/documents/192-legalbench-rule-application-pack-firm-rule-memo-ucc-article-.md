# legalbench-rule-application pack — Firm rule memo: UCC Article 2 scope versus common law

WHITLOCK & DEENE LLP — INTERNAL RULE MEMORANDUM (Commercial Group)
Re: Determining whether a contract is governed by UCC Article 2 or by the common law

Section 1. The rule. UCC Article 2 governs contracts for the sale of goods. Goods are movable, tangible things — canned food, machinery, vehicles, packaging, grain. The common law governs contracts for services, real estate, employment, construction on land, and intangibles such as intellectual property licenses. The classification question is purely about subject matter, not about price, sophistication of the parties, or whether the deal was written.

Section 2. Clean cases. A contract whose entire subject is the transfer of title to identified movable tangible items for a price is governed by Article 2. A contract whose entire subject is the performance of labor, consulting, design, or advisory work is governed by the common law, even when the service provider incidentally uses or delivers paper, software printouts, or other trivial materials while performing.

Section 3. Hybrid contracts — predominant purpose test. Many contracts mix goods and services (equipment plus installation; software plus training; food supply plus delivery staffing). For hybrid contracts the firm applies the predominant purpose test: classify the contract according to its dominant thrust. Our operational rule, which associates must apply mechanically when the letter supplies a price allocation: if MORE THAN HALF of the total contract price is attributable to the goods component, the ENTIRE contract is governed by UCC Article 2; if half or more of the price is attributable to services, the entire contract is governed by the common law. Do not split a single contract into two regimes.

Section 4. Vocabulary for the file. When recording a scope determination in the matter system, the firm uses a binary vocabulary: a determination that Article 2 governs is recorded as the affirmative disposition; a determination that the common law governs is recorded as the negative disposition. The recording clerk's instructions in each assignment control the exact strings.

Section 5. Traps to avoid. (a) The presence of a large dollar figure does not make a services contract a goods contract. (b) Installation and training obligations do not remove an equipment sale from Article 2 when the price allocation shows the goods predominate. (c) Real estate purchases are never Article 2 transactions regardless of what sits on the land. Questions on this memo go to the Commercial Group knowledge partner.
